1. What is CICOT?

CICOT stands for Central Islamic Council of Thailand. The organization's official website states that CICOT was established under the Islamic Religious Organization Administration Act B.E. 2540 (1997), and one of its main missions is to issue halal food product certificates.

In simple terms, CICOT is Thailand's central Islamic authority that Thai people and businesses use as a reference for halal standards in the country. When a restaurant, factory, or catering provider says they are "halal certified," the next question should not stop at whether they are certified — you should ask certified by whom, what is the number, and where can it be verified.

FACT

The CICOT about page on the official website states that the Central Islamic Council of Thailand consists of representatives from 40 Provincial Islamic Committees, one per province, each serving a 6-year term.

2. What Does a CICOT Halal Certificate Tell Food Buyers?

A CICOT certificate does not just mean the menu has no pork. It means the establishment has passed evaluation on ingredients, production processes, and conditions related to halal standards as defined by the authority. This makes the certificate far more valuable than an advertisement or a verbal promise in a sales chat.

For corporate food buyers, this is crucial. When Muslim employees or guests attend your event, you need to be able to explain how you selected the vendor. If the vendor has certified documents, you can attach a copy for procurement, management, or the event owner to review in their decision-making.

3. How Does the CICOT Halal Certification Process Work?

The guidelines and requirements published by CICOT show that certification is not superficial but a multi-step process. Here is a simple summary:

1
Submit Application and Document Review

The applicant must submit a completed application form with supporting documents. If documents are incomplete, the guidelines state that officers will contact the applicant and allow 7 business days to submit additional documents.

2
Pay Fees and Attend Training

The guidelines state that new applicants may be required to attend halal training before the inspection to understand the requirements and practical guidelines.

3
On-Site Establishment Inspection

An on-site inspection is conducted to examine the kitchen, equipment, processes, and on-site readiness. According to the guidelines, inspections are typically scheduled 1-2 weeks after payment.

4
Correct Deficiencies and Submit to Committee

If serious deficiencies are found, the guidelines specify a 30-day correction period before results are submitted for approval of the halal certification mark at the monthly meeting of the Central Islamic Council of Thailand.

WHY THIS MATTERS TO BUYERS

The more you understand how many steps are required to obtain a certificate, the clearer the difference becomes between a vendor with verified certification and one that uses the word "halal" without supporting documents.

4. What Must a Vendor Do After Receiving Certification?

Requirement R-CICOT-01 of the Halal Affairs Department states that certified parties must maintain halal standards throughout the certification period and must consent to follow-up inspections. They must also have a traceability system for certified products, including production date, production location, expiry date, labels, and certificate number.

From the perspective of a corporate food buyer, this makes a halal certificate far more valuable than a storefront logo — because behind it is a system that must be maintained continuously, not just a one-time document for marketing purposes.

5. How to Verify if a Vendor's Certificate is Reliable

  • Check that the business name and certificate number are clearly visible, not just a cropped logo image
  • Look for the expiry date or validity period of the certificate
  • Request a full copy of the certificate, especially for corporate work with internal approval processes
  • Search the reference number in the halal product system at `halal.co.th` when sufficient data is available
  • Ask about the scope of certification — does it actually cover the service you are purchasing

If a vendor avoids sending documents, only sends a small unreadable image, or says "trust us" without a reference number, that is a signal to stop and investigate further before making payment.
